Frequently Asked Questions

Compliance & Jurisdictional FAQs

Operating under the Pennsylvania Revised Uniform Law on Notarial Acts (RULONA).

Are Pennsylvania Remote Online Notarizations recognized in other states?

Yes. Notarial acts performed by a commissioned Pennsylvania notary under PA RULONA are generally recognized across the United States under interstate recognition statutes and the Full Faith and Credit Clause (U.S. Const. art. IV, § 1). However, acceptance policies for specific document types (such as electronic deeds or testamentary wills) can vary by county recorder, court, or lender. Signers should confirm acceptance with their receiving institution prior to signing.

How does Form I-9 verification differ from an official notarial act?

Completing Section 2 of Form I-9 is an employment eligibility verification governed by federal USCIS law. The notary acts strictly as the employer's designated "Authorized Representative." Under Pennsylvania law, notaries are expressly prohibited from applying an official notary stamp or seal to Form I-9. Remote examination is available for eligible employers under authorized DHS procedures (such as E-Verify enrollment).

What identity proofing is required for remote signers?

Signers must pass automated Credential Analysis (biometric scanning and security inspection of an unexpired government photo ID) and dynamic Knowledge-Based Authentication (KBA, answering 5 public-records questions in under 2 minutes with >= 80% score) prior to entering the live BlueNotary video session.

How does Pennsylvania Apostille courier filing work?

Our courier conducts a preliminary document and seal audit, hand-delivers your dossier directly to the Pennsylvania Department of State Bureau of Elections in Harrisburg for expedited processing, retrieves the certified dossier in person upon completion, and dispatches it directly to you via tracked Priority Overnight courier.

How long are video recordings retained?

In strict compliance with 57 Pa.C.S. § 319, complete audiovisual recordings of every remote online session and electronic journal logs are maintained in secure, tamper-evident cloud repositories for a mandatory minimum period of ten (10) years.
