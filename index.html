Compliance & Jurisdictional FAQs

Operating under the Pennsylvania Revised Uniform Law on Notarial Acts (RULONA).

Are Pennsylvania Remote Online Notarizations recognized across all 50 states?

Yes. Under the Full Faith and Credit Clause of the United States Constitution (Article IV, Section 1) and state-level interstate recognition laws, notarial acts properly performed by a commissioned Pennsylvania notary under PA RULONA carry full legal validity and enforceability across all 50 states.

How does Form I-9 verification differ from an official notarial act?

Completing Section 2 of Form I-9 is an employment eligibility verification governed by federal USCIS law. The notary acts strictly as an "Authorized Representative" of the hiring company. Under Pennsylvania law, notaries are expressly prohibited from applying an official notary stamp or seal to Form I-9.

What identity proofing is required for remote signers?

Signers must pass automated Credential Analysis (biometric scanning and security-feature analysis of an unexpired government-issued photo ID) followed by dynamic Knowledge-Based Authentication (KBA) challenge questions sourced from public and credit records before entering the live audiovisual session.

How long are session recordings retained?

In strict compliance with 57 Pa.C.S. § 319, full audiovisual recordings of every remote session and electronic journal logs are stored in secure, tamper-evident cloud repositories for a mandatory period of ten (10) years.
